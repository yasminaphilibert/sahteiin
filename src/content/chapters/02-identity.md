The mark is two strokes leaning together, a spark where they touch. It is the
sound of the brand made visible — the clink of two glasses — and it lives
inside the wordmark itself, as the doubled I of SAHTEIIN. On the box it is
printed in gloss varnish on matte plum, so it only appears when the light
moves. A logo you notice the way you notice a toast: at the moment it happens.

The Latin and the Arabic are drawn as equals. صحتين is not a translation
tucked under the English — it is set large, as a graphic element in its own
right, because in Beirut it is the word people will actually say. Two scripts,
one weight, neither explaining the other.

---

The rule for everything in this chapter is bar, not pharmacy. Premium,
colourful, a little cheeky — dark plum as the home ground, bone type, one
amber rim light. The seven flavour colours belong to the sticks and never to
the masterbrand, so the brand stays composed while the range gets to be loud.
You see the direction early and shape it with me — that is the "look" decision,
and it happens here.
